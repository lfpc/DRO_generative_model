\title{Robustness of the SHiP muon shield to parameter tolerances}
\author{}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We study how sensitive the number of muons reaching the SHiP sensitive plane is to small
deviations of the 43 free parameters of the optimised muon shield from their nominal values.
A neural surrogate of the per-muon hit probability (LCSO, an ensemble of four DeepONets) gives
the gradient, the Hessian, the active subspace and candidate worst-case directions; every
conclusion is checked with the full simulation on $10^9$ muons. The surrogate is integrated over
the muon flow on a quadrature grid rather than sampled, so its numbers carry no sampling noise. The surrogate is validated
against the simulator on 158 reference designs and reproduces every one-at-a-time effect to
about $3\%$; along the worst directions it now predicts the simulated magnitudes, not only the
directions. At the nominal design the simulator gives $803\pm28$ hits per $10^9$ muons. If every
parameter may deviate by up to $\varepsilon$ of its nominal value, the worst combination raises
the hits by a factor $1.41\pm0.07$ at $\varepsilon=1\%$, $1.94\pm0.08$ at $2\%$, $3.1\pm0.1$ at
$3\%$, $7.7\pm0.3$ at $5\%$ and $86\pm3$ at $10\%$; the worst design found in the $\pm10\%$ box
gives $\times1150\pm40$. A single parameter at its $\pm10\%$ limit gives at most $2.3\pm0.1$ (the
field of M7). Random, independent deviations are benign at $\pm2\%$, mostly benign at $\pm5\%$, and
have a heavy tail at $\pm10\%$. The harmful directions all act through muons of
$30$--$75$\,GeV, whose hits rise by up to three orders of magnitude, while the muons above
$150$\,GeV respond oppositely.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup}
%==============================================================================

\paragraph{The shield.}
The design is the optimised configuration (\texttt{try\_opt}) of seven magnets M1--M7 (M0 is
fixed). Of its parameters, 43 are free (Table~\ref{tab:params}): magnet lengths
\texttt{Z\_len} and gaps between magnets \texttt{Z\_gap}, the transverse sizes
\texttt{dXIn}, \texttt{dXOut}, the inner gaps \texttt{gapIn}, \texttt{gapOut}, the yoke widths
\texttt{dXYokeIn/Out} and the field target $B$ of each magnet (the \texttt{NI} column of
the parametrisation, which is the target field in tesla since the currents are derived from it).
Lengths are in cm. The field is simulated as uniform within each magnet block, with the
cavern included.

\paragraph{Tolerances.}
Each parameter $\phi_i$ is allowed to vary by $\pm10\%$ of its nominal value $\phi_{0,i}$. We
use the normalised coordinate
\begin{equation}
  \phi_i = \phi_{0,i}\,(1 + 0.1\,u_i), \qquad u_i\in[-1,1],
\end{equation}
so $u=0$ is the nominal design and $u_i=\pm1$ puts parameter $i$ alone at its tolerance limit.
A step of length $\|u\|=1$ in any direction is ``one tolerance unit'': the same size of change
as one parameter moved to its limit, spread over all parameters. The corners of the box are
at $\|u\|=\sqrt{43}=6.6$. For M6 and M7 the field is negative, so a negative relative change
of their $B$ lowers $|B|$.

\paragraph{Muons and figure of merit.}
Muons are drawn from the current generative flow of \texttt{generator\_muons} at temperature
$T=1$, i.e.\ the physical flux, placed on axis at $z=-1$. The figure of merit is the number of
muons $H$ crossing the last sensitive plane (position 91) per $10^9$ muons. At the nominal design
the hit rate is $8\times10^{-7}$ per muon ($803\pm28$ and $817\pm29$ hits in two runs on the
$10^9$ muons of the analysis, $820\pm14$ on the independent $10^9$ muons of the validation of
Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}; the surrogate gives $768$). Every simulator number in this report uses
$10^9$ muons, the same muons for all designs.
(An earlier version of this study used an older flow, which gave $368$ hits per $10^9$; all
numbers here are new.)

\section{The surrogate}
%==============================================================================

The surrogate (LCSO) models the hit probability of each muon $x$ for a design $u$,
\begin{equation}
  p(\text{hit}\mid x,u) = \sigma\!\big(b(u)\cdot t(x) + c\big),
\end{equation}
with a branch network $b$ (the design) and a trunk network $t$ (the muon's $p_x$, $p_y$,
$p_z$ and charge). The expected number of hits is $H(u)=\sum_x p(\text{hit}\mid x,u)$, which is
smooth, so its gradient and Hessian with respect to $u$ are exact derivatives of the
surrogate, computed in one pass over the muons.

\paragraph{Training data.}
Designs are simulated once, in pools, and every surrogate is fitted to them. Each design has
its own muons from the flow, at a raised \emph{momentum} temperature $T$ (only the momentum part
of the flow is tempered): this broadens the momentum spectrum and raises the hit rate from
$8\times10^{-7}$ ($T=1$) to $1.3\times10^{-4}$ ($T=1.5$) and $1.7\times10^{-3}$ ($T=2$). It does not
bias the model, which learns the conditional probability $p(\text{hit}\mid x,u)$ per muon. Every
hit is kept, and $0.4\%$ of the misses; the logit is fitted with the offset $\log(1/0.004)$, so
the model remains one of the full probability. The pools (all with designs drawn in the box,
pulled towards the nominal design by a uniform radial factor):

\paragraph{The model.}
Four DeepONets (two hidden layers of 128 units, 32 branch/trunk features, $47\,553$ parameters
each) are trained with different seeds on all the pools at once ($2.1\times10^8$ rows), with
Adam, early stopping on held-out designs and the learning rate divided by 3 at each plateau.
The four logits are averaged; the average is itself a DeepONet (branch and trunk features
concatenated), so it is used exactly like a single model. How these choices were made is the
subject of Sec.~\ref{sec:study}.

\paragraph{Are the derivatives determined?}
The gradient at $u=0$ of the four members agrees to $\cos=0.94$--$0.98$ ($0.985$--$0.997$ on its
ten largest components, which carry $90\%$ of its norm), and their top Hessian eigenvector to
$0.96$--$0.99$; the second and third eigenvectors are nearly degenerate and are not individually
determined. The earlier surrogate's gradient is at $\cos=0.55$ from these.

\subsection{Integrating the surrogate over the muons}
\label{sec:quadrature}

The surrogate's expected hits are an integral over the muon flow, which until now was estimated
by sampling $10^9$ muons. With the muons on axis, a muon is its momentum, azimuth and charge, and
in the flow these are independent: $(p_z,p_t)$ is the image of a two-dimensional standard normal
latent $z$ under the momentum flow, the azimuth $\varphi$ is uniform and the charge is $\mu^+$ with
the data's fraction. So, with $A(z)$ the kinematic envelope of the sampled muons and $N=10^9$,
\begin{equation}
  H(u) = \frac{N}{\int\!\mathrm{d}^2z\,\mathcal{N}(z)A(z)}\sum_{q=\pm1}P(q)\int\!\mathrm{d}^2z\,
  \mathcal{N}(z)\,A(z)\int_0^{2\pi}\!\frac{\mathrm{d}\varphi}{2\pi}\,p\big(\text{hit}\mid u, x(z,\varphi,q)\big),
\end{equation}
which is integrated on a grid: $801^2$ latent points on $[-7,7]^2$ with Gaussian weights, 64
azimuths and both charges for the derivatives, and $201^2\times64\times2$ points, whose trunk
features are cached, for everything else. Figure~\ref{fig:quadrature} compares this with the
sampling used until now: the grid is converged to $10^{-5}$ from about $10^6$ points, while $10^9$
sampled muons still scatter by $\pm2\%$ in $H_0$ and by $\pm5\%$ in the hits above $300$\,GeV, and
quasi-Monte Carlo (Sobol points through the flow) helps only by a factor of two. The grid also
takes seconds instead of the 20 minutes of a $10^9$-muon pass. All surrogate numbers below are such
integrals; their errors are the standard error of the four ensemble members, i.e.\ the surrogate's
own uncertainty. Every simulator number still uses sampled muons; sampling them at a raised
temperature and reweighting does not help, because the hits that matter are at intermediate
momenta, which a hotter sample holds fewer of.

\section{How the surrogate was chosen}
\label{sec:study}
%==============================================================================

The choices above come from a study of the surrogates themselves (\texttt{surrogate\_study/}):
on four toy problems with the same structure (a binary outcome per sample with a known
probability, so the hit rate, its gradient and its Hessian are exact; 10--43 parameters) and on
the muon shield against the reference designs. Its conclusions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Modelling each muon is what makes it work.} A response surface fitted to the
        same simulations without the muons needs about ten times more simulation for the same
        gradient (Fig.~\ref{fig:study}, left).
  \item \textbf{Architecture.} The DeepONet is the most reliable for the hit rate. Explicit
        Taylor surrogates (a per-muon quadratic) give the best Hessians when the response has
        low-rank structure, best of all when restricted to a low-dimensional subspace; full-rank
        quadratics and residual corrections add parameters and lose accuracy. The branch
        activation matters (ReLU is worst, having no curvature of its own), width and depth
        hardly, except with the $\sim10^8$ rows of the final training, where a larger network
        (128 units, 32 features) clearly helps and averaging seeds removes most of the
        remaining scatter of the gradient.
  \item \textbf{Training.} Many designs with few muons each beat few designs with many; all
        designs at once beat a rolling buffer of designs; and the training designs must cover
        the region where derivatives are read. On the shield, a hotter momentum temperature
        ($T=2$) is much better than $T=1$ or $1.5$, and $T=3$ is no better than $T=2$
        (Fig.~\ref{fig:study}, right).
  \item \textbf{Directions are robust, magnitudes are not quadratic.} The gradient and Hessian
        give the right worst directions, but the response grows faster than quadratically away
        from $u=0$; every magnitude below is therefore a simulated one.
\end{itemize}

\section{Method}
%==============================================================================

Integrating the surrogate over the muon flow at $T=1$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:quadrature}) we compute:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the gradient $g=\nabla_u H$ and the Hessian $\nabla^2_u H$ at $u=0$;
  \item the exact one-at-a-time (OAT) change $H(\pm e_i)/H_0-1$ of each parameter at its
        tolerance limit;
  \item the active subspace, the eigenvectors of $C=\mathbb{E}_x[\nabla_u p\,\nabla_u p^\top]$,
        i.e.\ the directions in which the hit probability of individual muons changes most;
  \item candidate worst directions, each normalised to $\|v\|=1$:
    \texttt{ascent} ($v\propto g$),
    \texttt{worst} (the maximiser of the quadratic model $g\cdot v+\tfrac12 v^\top\nabla^2H\,v$ on
    $\|v\|=1$, a trust-region problem),
    \texttt{hess1} (top Hessian eigenvector), \texttt{act1}, \texttt{act2} (top active
    directions), \texttt{corner} ($v\propto\operatorname{sign} g$, every parameter moved by the
    same amount), \texttt{random}, and
    \texttt{pga} (projected-gradient ascent of the surrogate over the whole $\pm10\%$ box);
  \item 4096 designs drawn uniformly in the box.
\end{itemize}
Errors on surrogate quantities are the standard error of the four ensemble members
(standard deviation$/\sqrt4$); there is no sampling error. Each direction is then ``walked'': the
designs $u=t\,v$ for $t\in\{-1,-\tfrac12,0,\tfrac12,1\}$ (for \texttt{pga}, up to the box face)
are simulated on the same $10^9$ sampled muons. Simulator errors are Poisson.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Single parameters}

37 of the 43 one-at-a-time effects are resolved beyond three times the members' scatter
(Fig.~\ref{fig:sensitivity}); the largest are in Table~\ref{tab:oat}. The most sensitive parameter is
the \emph{field of M7}: lowering $|B|$ by $10\%$ doubles the hits ($+96\pm1\%$). Next come the
entrance width of M2 (\texttt{dXIn} $-10\%$: $+77\%$), the length of M7 ($-10\%$: $+58\%$), the yoke widths of M7
and M3, the exit width of M7 and the length of M6. Most responses are strongly asymmetric: one
side of the tolerance hardly matters, the other is harmful. Some parameters (the lengths of M2
and M3, the field of M3) are harmful on \emph{both} sides: the nominal value sits at their
minimum. Per magnet (Fig.~\ref{fig:magnets}), M7 carries by far the largest share of the
gradient ($79\%$ of its norm), followed by M2 ($40\%$), M3 ($30\%$) and M6 ($26\%$); M5 and M1
matter least. The scans of Fig.~\ref{fig:scans} show the full one-dimensional responses.

These numbers are now reliable: the eight largest were simulated (Sec.~\ref{sec:concrete}),
and the simulator agrees within $1$--$2\sigma$ for six of them (e.g.\ M7:\texttt{Z\_len} $-10\%$:
simulator $\times1.65\pm0.07$, surrogate $\times1.58$); the field of M7 ($2.26\pm0.10$ vs.\ $1.96$)
and the exit width of M7 ($1.85\pm0.08$ vs.\ $1.43$) are underestimated.

\subsection{Curvature and active subspace}

One Hessian eigenvalue dominates (Fig.~\ref{fig:spectra}): $3.25\pm0.09\,H_0$ per unit$^2$, then
$1.01$, $0.76$ and $\lesssim0.34\,H_0$; 12 are negative, down to only $-0.10\,H_0$, and 31 of the 43
are resolved beyond the members' scatter. Along the gradient the second-order term is already
larger than the first-order one at one tolerance unit ($1.10\,H_0$ vs.\ $0.93\,H_0$), so the response is strongly non-linear at that
scale.

The active subspace is effectively one-dimensional: its first direction carries $99.1\%$ of
$\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla_u p\|^2]$. That direction, \texttt{act1}, shortens M1--M4 and lowers their
fields together (Table~\ref{tab:dirs}): it changes the bending power upstream. It is almost
orthogonal to the gradient ($\cos=0.07$): it is the direction in which individual muons are
deflected most, but those changes largely cancel in the total count.

\paragraph{Small coordinated changes are far more harmful than a large single one.}
Moving every parameter by at most $4.1\%$ along \texttt{worst} (RMS $1.5\%$) multiplies the hits
by $5.2\pm0.2$: $4263$ vs.\ $817$. The simplest such design, \texttt{corner}, moves \emph{every}
parameter by exactly $1.5\%$ in the direction of the sign of its gradient and doubles the hits
($2.08\pm0.09$). The worst single parameter needs a $10\%$ error for a comparable effect (M7:$B$,
$2.26\pm0.10$). A random direction of the same size changes nothing ($0.96\pm0.05$): the damage
comes from the specific pattern of signs, not from the size of the step.

\paragraph{The surrogate finds the right directions, and now the right sizes.}
Its two best directions (\texttt{worst}, \texttt{hess1}, with $\cos=0.98$ between them) are the
two that the simulator ranks highest, followed by \texttt{ascent}. Unlike the earlier surrogate,
which overestimated these effects by a factor $1.8$, the final one is within $1$--$2\sigma$ of
the simulator, within the members' scatter and the Poisson noise combined, for every design
within one tolerance unit except \texttt{act2} at $t=1$ ($1.19$ vs.\ $1.46\pm0.07$) and the
$t=-1$ ends of \texttt{worst}, \texttt{hess1} and \texttt{ascent}, where it is $20$--$30\%$ high
(Fig.~\ref{fig:calibration}). It fails only for the extreme box designs, where its members
themselves disagree by orders of magnitude (Sec.~\ref{sec:box}).

\paragraph{The quadratic model is not enough.}
The quadratic model built from the gradient and Hessian underestimates the rise along every
harmful direction ($3.4$ instead of $5.2$ along \texttt{worst}, Fig.~\ref{fig:walks}): the hits
grow roughly exponentially. The derivatives give the directions; the magnitudes come from the
surrogate itself and from the simulator.

\paragraph{The active subspace is not where the hits rise most.}
\texttt{act1} gives $2.50\pm0.10$ at $t=+1$, against $5.2$ for \texttt{worst}. It is a direction
of curvature, not of slope: the simulator gives a rise on \emph{both} sides ($1.60\pm0.07$ at
$t=-1$). Shortening (or lengthening) magnets 1--4 together, with their fields, is therefore
harmful either way; the nominal design sits at the balance point (Sec.~\ref{sec:momentum}). The
plane spanned by \texttt{worst} and \texttt{act1} (Fig.~\ref{fig:plane}) shows the nominal
design in a shallow valley that rises in most directions.

\subsection{The worst case in the full tolerance box}
\label{sec:box}

Projected-gradient ascent of the surrogate over the whole $\pm10\%$ box ends at a corner, with
all 43 parameters at a limit. The surrogate predicts $\HH=(7\pm5)\times10^4$ there: extrapolation
far outside its training data, on which its members disagree by orders of magnitude. But the
design is genuinely catastrophic: the simulator gives \textbf{$924\,925$ hits, $\HH=1150\pm40$}.
Halfway there (every parameter at $5\%$) the simulator gives $\HH=36.7\pm1.3$ (surrogate
$68\pm24$), and even the opposite corner is bad, $\HH=12.6\pm0.5$ (surrogate $7.9\pm2.5$). Almost all of its hits are muons of $40$--$60$\,GeV
(Sec.~\ref{sec:momentum}). The corner moves all 43 parameters to a limit at once: it shows how
far $\pm10\%$ tolerances can go, not a likely configuration.

\paragraph{How the risk grows with the tolerance.}
Along the worst direction (Fig.~\ref{fig:scaled}) the hits grow by $\times1.4$ at a maximum change
of $1\%$, $\times1.9$ at $2\%$, $\times3.1$ at $3\%$, $\times7.7$ at $5\%$ and $\times86$ at $10\%$:
roughly exponentially, a factor $e$ per $\sim2.2\%$ of tolerance. The opposite direction is
harmless at $5\%$ ($\times1.2$) but bad at $10\%$ ($\times15$), through other muons
(Sec.~\ref{sec:momentum}). The surrogate follows the simulator to within $10\%$ up to $5\%$
tolerance and within $12\%$ at $10\%$; on the opposite side it underestimates the $10\%$ point by a
factor two.

\paragraph{The rise is collective.}
The largest component alone (M2:\texttt{dXIn} $-10\%$) gives $\times1.9$; the top three
(adding a weaker and shorter M7) $\times7$; the top five $\times15$; the top ten $\times40$; all 43
$\times86$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:attribution}). Each parameter contributes a moderate factor, and the
factors multiply.

\paragraph{Single parameters.}

($\times2.26\pm0.10$), the entrance width of M2 ($\times1.89$), the exit width of M7 ($\times1.85$)
and the length of M7 ($\times1.65$). The surrogate's ranking is confirmed: all eight it puts first
are simulated with a significant rise ($\ge6.4\sigma$), with magnitudes within $1$--$2\sigma$ except
the field ($1.96$ predicted) and the exit width of M7 ($1.43$).

\subsection{Dependence on the muon momentum}
\label{sec:momentum}

The muons are split into bins of $p_z$ (and, separately, of $p_t$ and charge) and the hit count
$H_b$ of each bin is analysed on its own: its gradient, its one-at-a-time effects relative to its
own nominal hits, and its own worst direction. This uses the surrogate integrated over the flow,
so even the sparse high-momentum bins have no sampling noise; the per-bin values below $75$\,GeV
carry its $T=1$ uncertainty (Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}), so the
\emph{directions} and \emph{signs} are the robust part. The simulated hits per $p_z$ range of the
concrete designs (Fig.~\ref{fig:simpz}) confirm the picture directly.

\paragraph{Three populations of hits.}
Most muons are soft ($50\%$ have $p_z<30$\,GeV), but at the nominal design the hits come from
two groups (Table~\ref{tab:pz}; simulated shares in parentheses):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{intermediate momenta}, $p_z=30$--$75$\,GeV: $64\%$ of the hits ($67\%$ simulated,
        $42\%$ at $40$--$50$\,GeV alone), at a hit rate of $10^{-6}$ per muon;
  \item \emph{high momenta}, $p_z>125$\,GeV: $1.2\%$ of the muons but $27\%$ of the hits
        ($23\%$ simulated), with hit rates rising to $10^{-3}$ per muon above $300$\,GeV. These are
        the muons the shield does not deflect enough.
\end{itemize}
A third group, \emph{soft} muons of $20$--$30$\,GeV, gives only $4\%$ of the hits at the nominal
design but responds strongly to some changes. The $75$--$125$\,GeV range contributes $4\%$.

\paragraph{The populations want opposite changes.}
The worst directions of the $30$--$45$ and $45$--$75$\,GeV bins agree ($\cos=0.92$), and so do
those of all bins above $125$\,GeV ($0.82$--$1.00$), but the two groups are anti-aligned
($\cos=-0.38$ to $-0.53$), and the soft $10$--$30$\,GeV bins are anti-aligned with the
intermediate ones too ($\cos$ down to $-0.71$, Fig.~\ref{fig:pz_alignment}). The total gradient,
and hence every harmful direction of Table~\ref{tab:walks}, follows the intermediate-momentum
group ($\cos=0.83$--$0.88$). Along the global \texttt{worst} direction the hits of the
$30$--$75$\,GeV bins rise by $144$--$205\%$ per tolerance unit (first order), while those of
$20$--$30$\,GeV fall by $165\%$ and those above $300$\,GeV by $112\%$.

Simulated (Fig.~\ref{fig:simpz}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item along the worst direction at $10\%$, the $40$--$50$\,GeV hits go from $335$ to
        $49\,480$ and the $50$--$60$\,GeV ones from $55$ to $16\,769$, while the hits above
        $150$\,GeV \emph{fall} from $186$ to $58$ and those below $30$\,GeV from $35$ to $9$;
  \item along the opposite direction the $40$--$50$\,GeV hits fall from $335$ to $82$, but those
        of $20$--$30$\,GeV rise from $34$ to $9700$ and those above $150$\,GeV from $186$ to
        $1246$: the opposite direction is harmful through the other two populations;
  \item the box worst case acts only at $30$--$75$\,GeV ($541\to923\,780$ hits), leaving the
        high-momentum hits unchanged ($186\to59$);
  \item random $\pm10\%$ designs can open any of them: one raises the $40$--$50$\,GeV hits from
        $335$ to $3144$, another those above $150$\,GeV from $186$ to $1028$, and the worst of the
        eight ($\times7.9$) both the soft ($35\to2045$) and the high-momentum ($186\to3335$) ones.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Which parameters act on which muons} (Fig.~\ref{fig:pz_sensitivity}).
The high-momentum hits are controlled by the \emph{upstream} magnets: the lengths of M1--M4 and
their fields, each of which changes the hits above $175$\,GeV by $30$--$210\%$ at its worse
limit. The intermediate-momentum hits are controlled by the entrance width of M2, the
yoke widths of M3 and M7, and above all by the \emph{downstream} magnet M7 (its field: $+155\%$ of
the $30$--$75$\,GeV hits at the worse limit). The soft $20$--$30$\,GeV hits respond most to the
entrance widths of M2 and M3 and the yoke of M3 (up to $\times7$). This trade-off explains the
curvature direction \texttt{act1}: changing the length and field of M1--M4 together shifts hits
between the high-momentum group and the others, and the nominal design balances them, so
either sign increases the total ($\times1.60$ and $\times2.50$ at $t=\mp1$).

\paragraph{Transverse momentum.}
Split in $p_t$, all bins below $1.5$\,GeV behave alike (their worst directions agree to
$\cos=0.89$--$1.00$, with the same leading parameters as the total). The muons with
$p_t>1.5$\,GeV ($1.6\%$ of the muons, $24\%$ of the hits) are different: their worst direction is
only partly aligned with the others ($\cos=0.55$--$0.65$) and they are less sensitive
($|\nabla H_b|/H_b=0.81$ vs.\ $1.0$--$1.2$, Fig.~\ref{fig:pt}).

\paragraph{Charge.}
Positive muons give $63\%$ of the hits (hit rate $8.9\times10^{-7}$ against $6.2\times10^{-7}$
for $\mu^-$). Unlike in the earlier version of this study, both charges respond to the same
changes: their worst directions agree to $\cos=0.97$, the $\mu^-$ being somewhat more sensitive
($1.10$ vs.\ $0.85$ per tolerance unit).

\section{Conclusions}
%==============================================================================

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The design is fragile to coordinated deviations.} If every parameter may be off
        by up to $\varepsilon$, the worst combination raises the hits by $\times1.4$ at
        $\varepsilon=1\%$, $\times1.9$ at $2\%$, $\times3.1$ at $3\%$, $\times7.7$ at $5\%$ and
        $\times86$ at $10\%$, roughly a factor $e$ per $2.2\%$; the worst design found in the
        $\pm10\%$ box gives $\times1150$. Moving every parameter by $1.5\%$ in the sign of its
        gradient already doubles the hits.
  \item \textbf{A single parameter needs a much larger error for the same effect}: at its full
        $\pm10\%$ limit the worst one (the field of M7) gives $\times2.3$; the entrance width of M2,
        the exit width and the length of M7 give $\times1.65$--$1.9$. Tolerances specified
        parameter by parameter therefore underestimate the risk by orders of magnitude.
  \item \textbf{Random deviations are much less dangerous than the worst case}: independent
        errors of $\pm2\%$ on every parameter keep the hits within about $\pm20\%$; at $\pm5\%$
        most designs stay within $\pm60\%$ but some quadruple the hits; at $\pm10\%$ the median
        design is twice as bad and the tail reaches $\times8$--$28$.
  \item \textbf{The rise is collective}: the top three changes of the worst direction (a narrower
        M2 entrance, a weaker and shorter M7) give $\times7$, the top ten $\times40$, all 43
        $\times86$. M7 matters most, then M2, M3 and M6.
  \item \textbf{Different muons pull in opposite directions.} The hits come from muons of
        $30$--$75$\,GeV ($67\%$, simulated) and above $150$\,GeV ($23\%$). Every harmful
        direction found acts through the first group, whose hits rise up to a thousandfold,
        while the high-momentum hits fall; reversing it raises the soft ($20$--$30$\,GeV) and
        high-momentum hits instead.
  \item \textbf{The active subspace is not a worst-case direction}: the dominant one (the
        lengths and fields of M1--M4 together) is a direction of curvature, harmful on both
        sides, which trades hits between the momentum populations.
  \item \textbf{The surrogate is now quantitative within the tolerance region.} Trained on
        $5\times10^{10}$ simulated muons at raised momentum temperature, with four averaged
        networks, it reproduces the simulator's one-at-a-time effects to $3\%$ and the walks
        within one tolerance unit to $1$--$2\sigma$, and its worst-direction magnitudes up to $10\%$
        tolerance to $\sim10\%$. It still fails at the box corners (orders of magnitude) and is
        $\sim15\%$ uncertain at $T=1$ far from the nominal design; every number used for a
        decision is simulated.
  \item \textbf{Integrate the surrogate, do not sample it.} Over the muon flow's latent space a
        quadrature grid gives the surrogate's hits, derivatives and per-momentum splits to
        $10^{-5}$ in seconds, where $10^9$ sampled muons leave $2$--$5\%$ of noise; what remains is
        the surrogate's own uncertainty, estimated from the scatter of its ensemble members.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Limitations}
Uniform fields per magnet block (no field maps); muons from the generative flow, not the full
simulated sample, so the absolute numbers depend on the flow version (the nominal hit rate
changed by a factor 2.2 between flow versions); the surrogate's errors are the scatter of its
ensemble members, which measures its seed-to-seed uncertainty but not a bias they share (such as
the $T=1$ one of Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}); simulator errors are Poisson; the transport is stochastic, so this is appropriate even between
designs sharing the same muons. The second and later Hessian eigenvectors are not individually
determined by the surrogate. The random-tolerance distributions rest on eight simulated designs
per level and on the surrogate for their tails.
